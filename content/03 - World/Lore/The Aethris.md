# The Aethris

> 

---

## Overview

The Aethris were the mortal agents of [[Corvina]] — created to guide souls into and out of the [[Mortal Plane]] on her behalf. They were not immortal. They were not human. They were something in between: beings shaped for a specific sacred function, whose presence in the mortal realm was always temporary by design.

In the current era of the main campaign, the Aethris are gone. [[Corvina]] has been lost and reincorporated into [[Yaaw]], and without their patron, the Aethris have ceased to exist as a people.

See also: [[Corvina]], [[Yaaw]], [[The Hooded One]], [[Torris]], [[The Three Realms]]

---

## Nature and Appearance

The Aethris looked much like elves — slender, angular, with a similar physical grace. They were not given the elves' gift of immortality. Their lives in the mortal realm were described by Corvina as a *temporary lease* — they were always passing through, their presence here a brief assignment before return.

Their most distinctive feature was their **aura**. Each Aethris had an aura colored to reflect one of Corvina's six birds, corresponding to the specific task Corvina gave them at their creation:

- Stork, magpie, or pelican — life-bringing functions, guiding new souls in
- Raven, starling, or dove — soul-carrying functions, guiding the dead onward

At certain angles, within the light of the aura, **wings** could be seen spread across their backs — Corvina's wings, worn as a reflection of their purpose.

---

## Function

The Aethris' work was the same work Corvina did, scaled to the mortal plane's need for agents who could be present with the dying and the newly born. They did not cause death. They did not decide who died. They ensured that the passage was not made alone, and that the soul arrived at the [[The Hushed Lands|Hushed Lands]] coherently rather than fragmenting.

This made them, in cosmological terms, the mortal-plane equivalent of what the [[Lucitans]] were for fae — guides who ensured the dead were not lost. Both peoples were doing the same sacred work. Both were destroyed or diminished by the Elven Empire for the same underlying reason.

---

## Persecution

The Elven Empire forbade worship of [[Yaaw]] entirely. The theological reason was explicit: mortality was framed as the punishment of lesser beings, the justification for their station in servitude. A god who presided over death with dignity — who implied that the dead passed onward into something meaningful — was incompatible with this. If death was sacred rather than shameful, the entire architecture of elven supremacy began to crack.

The Aethris were the walking embodiment of this problem. They could not simply be ignored the way Yaaw could be officially unspeakable. They were present. They were visible. They were doing the work.

They were treated accordingly. The historical parallel is the persecution of folk healers, midwives, and those who tended the dead in periods of religious or political consolidation — people whose intimate knowledge of the body's thresholds made them powerful and therefore threatening. The Aethris were hunted as heretics, their practices criminalized, their community networks systematically dismantled.

**The Hooded One's warlock half was Aethris.** His persecution is not historical for him. It is personal. It is the wound that was still open when [[Corvina]]'s Thornwood patron found him.

---

## Mazz and the Hooded One

[[Mazz]] was an Aethris and one of the [[The Legendary Heroes|Legendary Heroes]] of the slave revolt. He was [[Corvina]]'s agent. He took the guiding of souls seriously in the way that only someone who had seen what happened to souls that weren't guided could.

His merger with the Thornwood Court patron — the process that created the Hooded One — severed his connection to Corvina. Whether this severing contributed to Corvina's eventual loss, or whether it was one wound among several that accumulated to the same end, is unresolved.

What is certain is that the Hooded One contains everything Mazz was, including the full weight of Aethris persecution and the intimate understanding of what souls need and what happens when they don't get it — twisted now by centuries of grief and hatred into something Corvina would not recognize.

---

## Torris

[[Torris]] was also Aethris — a cleric in the Legendary Heroes who took the function of soul-guiding with a particular seriousness that distinguished her from Mazz. Where Mazz carried the wound of persecution, Torris carried the obligation of the work itself. What became of her after the revolt, and whether her line contributed anything to the world that persists, is worth deciding.

---

## The Loss

The Aethris without Corvina are nothing — their existence was predicated on her. When she was lost and reabsorbed into Yaaw, the Aethris ceased. Whether this was simultaneous or whether some Aethris continued briefly, confused, without their patron before fading, is an open question.

The mortal souls that the Aethris would have guided are no longer being guided. This is a problem of the same scale as the unguided fae spirits in the volcano — and almost no one knows it exists.
