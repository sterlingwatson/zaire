# The Pyramid of First Anchoring

> *The elves built it to celebrate. They recorded everything they had learned about the courts, their seasons, their natures, their vulnerabilities. They called it scholarship. They called it achievement. They put it in stone so it would last forever.*

---

## Overview

The Pyramid of First Anchoring an elven monument in the [[Zaire]], built to commemorate the locking of the [[Fey Wild]] and to house the first anchor ever set. It was created height of the [[Elven Empire]].

The structure was built by elven mages who spent generations studying the [[The Four Diminished Courts]] before they were diminished; cataloguing them, mapping them, learning exactly how they worked so they could be suppressed. The pyramid is the record of that study and the instrument of that suppression, built as one thing.

The [[Anchors|anchor]] is still active inside. The [[Fey Wild]] presses against it from the other side of the [[Hedge]] like water against a dam. The whole structure hums with fae energy, not because the elves honored the fae, but because the fae cannot stop straining against the anchor here.

See also: [[Anchors]], [[The Four Diminished Courts]], [[The Hooded One]], [[The Hedge]], [[Talara]]

## History

The pyramid was constructed at the moment of maximum elven power, and it opened the first gate into the Fae from the [[Mortal Plane]] through [[The Hedge]].

The elven architects chose the site deliberately: this was the **sacred ground of the Chrysalis Court**. In the center of what is the [[Forsynth Desert]] used to lie the [[Great Tree]] of the [[Chrysalis Court]]. This first anchor was driven deep into the trunk of the [[Great Tree]] and the pyramid wanders, connected by a chain in the hope that it may work the roots of the tree out. 

The anchor was set during **Spring** — the Chrysalis Court's season of emergence and maximum magical activity. The elves chose this deliberately. They called it optimal timing. It was the moment of greatest vulnerability.

## The Murals

Each mural was made by elven scholars and reflects an elven perspective. They are detailed, accurate in many technical respects, and fundamentally incapable of seeing their subjects as complete.

### The Chrysalis Court Panel *(East Face)*
The most beautiful. The elves found the fae aesthetic, their metamorphosis was fascinating to elven naturalists. This panel is rendered with extraordinary care: transformation stages, wing geometries, the seasonal cycle of the court's magic. It reads like a field guide produced by someone who genuinely marveled at their subject, but also has the grim reality of a people hunted and cataloged.

The dead tree is depicted here. Marked as a site of power and a resource point for the anchor's placement. The firefly fae are shown in the lower register, also labeled. [[Talara]]'s people, documented and mapped by the architects of their diminishment.

The overall arrangement is taxonomy, not portraiture. The Chrysalis Court is rendered as *remarkable specimens* rather than a people.

### The Hollow Court Panel *(South Face)*
The most sparse. Goblins were not interesting to elven scholars in the way that the insectoid fae were. This panel is functional, closer to a ledger than art. Locations. Estimated populations. Notations about which communities were "secured" and when.

The notation style is identical to that used elsewhere in the pyramid for material resources and livestock.

If the goblin player looks closely, this should land without commentary from the GM.

### The Gilded Court Panel *(West Face)*
The most prominent, placed to be seen first by anyone approaching from the main path. The [[Gilded Court]] gnomes are depicted almost as peers — named figures in formal postures, the visual language of alliance and mutual achievement.

This was deliberate flattery. The elves wanted the Gilded Court to see this pyramid and feel honored by their inclusion. They very nearly succeeded.

The gnome figures are slightly smaller than the elven figures they stand beside. Whether this was conscious or not is left for the viewer to determine.

### The Thornwood Court Panel *(North Face)*
Set at the back, in shadow for most of the day. This panel reads as a **threat assessment document.** The hags, unicorns, and ancient things of the [[Thornwood Court]] are depicted with the visual vocabulary the elves used for dangerous magical entitiesm precise identification markings, capability notations, containment annotations.

One figure in the lower left is rendered with particular care and annotated extensively. The description, if translated from Elvish, is clinical and lengthy.

## Interior: The Calibration Chamber

The main interior chamber occupies most of the pyramid's volume. It is circular despite the square exterior, a deliberate architectural choice that signals the space operates by different logic than the rest of the structure.

The floor is three **concentric stone rings** surrounding a raised central dais. The anchor is sealed beneath the dais behind a **Crystalline Iris** — a locking mechanism of elven-fae hybrid construction, the one piece of the pyramid the elves did not build entirely from their own knowledge.

The air hums. Fae energy bleeds through the anchor constantly. The walls are warm to the touch despite the stone. In certain lights, the [[Chrysalis Court]]'s spring colours are faintly visible in the air — the anchor's suppression is not perfect, has never been perfect, and the Fey Wild remembering its own spring is the leak in the seal.

**The Hooded One's corruption is visible in the anchor from across the room.** Black veins thread through the Crystalline Iris like cracks. The anchor is doing two things at once: suppressing the Fey Wild as designed, and feeding the Hooded One's influence back through the Hedge toward [[Sehanine]].

## The Calibration Rings

The rings are not a fae ritual device. They are an **elven attunement mechanism.** When the anchor was first set, elven mages used these rings to calibrate its suppression to each court, demonstrating sufficient knowledge of each court's nature to lock it precisely. The rings allowed them to target the anchor's suppression with the specificity that made it so effective.

To open the Crystalline Iris and access the anchor, the mechanism must be run in reverse. The rings must be aligned to the **correct seasonal position for the court whose sacred ground this is.**

The correct alignment is the Chrysalis Court's: **Spring / Rain / Blossoms.**

The elves chose Spring because it was the moment of maximum vulnerability. To align the rings to Spring now is to acknowledge that choice and undo it.

### The Outer Ring — Climate
Four positions: Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter.

Corresponding courts as the elves mapped them:
- Spring → [[The Chrysalis Court]]
- Summer → [[The Hollow Court]]
- Autumn → [[The Gilded Court]]
- Winter → [[The Thornwood Court]]

**Current position when the party enters:** Autumn. The Gilded Court's season. The anchor was last calibrated here, during the height of the empire, when the collaboration was at its peak.

**The plinth inscription (in Elvish and Common):**
*"The outer mechanism turns for what was given before consent was possible."*

The elven intent was for their mages to demonstrate knowledge of a court's origin and nature. What the pyramid actually accepts is something more personal; a truth about what was decided for you before you had a voice.

When the ring moves toward Spring, the air softens. Something that might be a blossom scent. The carved dead tree above the apex door seems almost — not quite — to have something at its tip.

When the ring moves toward Winter, temperature drops sharply. The Thornwood imagery along the walls shifts in peripheral vision.

When the ring moves toward Summer, the room becomes thick and hot. The south wall's ledger-panel seems to pulse faintly.

When the ring moves toward Autumn, a smell of woodsmoke and decay. The Gilded Court panel seems to darken even in unchanged light.

### The Middle Ring — Element
Four positions: Rain, Heat, Wind, Ice.

Corresponding courts:
- Rain → [[The Chrysalis Court]] *(the chrysalis requires stillness and moisture)*
- Heat → [[The Hollow Court]] *(the fire of labor and forge)*
- Wind → [[The Gilded Court]] *(what carries away what you thought you owned)*
- Ice → [[The Thornwood Court]] *(preservation and killing as the same act)*

**The plinth inscription:**
*"The middle mechanism turns for what you are moving toward and what that movement requires you to leave."*

The elven intent was to demonstrate knowledge of a court's elemental nature. What the pyramid accepts is the confession of transformation — what you are becoming and what it will cost. The mechanism reads the emotional truth of what's said, not the words themselves.

Aligning this ring costs the speaker something regardless of success. The elven mages marked this in their records as an "acceptable toll." It is not a trap; it is simply what the mechanism requires.

### The Inner Ring — Flora
Four positions: Blossoms, Vines, Dead Wood, Evergreens.

Corresponding courts:
- Blossoms → [[The Chrysalis Court]]
- Vines → [[The Hollow Court]] *(growth that becomes chains)*
- Dead Wood → [[The Gilded Court]] *(their own end, recorded in their own season)*
- Evergreens → [[The Thornwood Court]] *(survival by refusing to change)*

**Current position:** Dead Wood. It has not moved since the Gilded Court fell.

**The plinth inscription:**
*"The inner mechanism turns for what cannot be excused. Only owned."*

The elven intent was to demonstrate complete mastery of a court's nature — the capstone of the attunement ritual. What the pyramid requires is accountability without justification. The mechanism is the most resistant of the three. It was designed to be operated by people with certainty. It does not know what to do with genuine reckoning, and it will resist before it yields.

**If no player can provide this authentically:** A faint impression of a [[Gilded Court]] gnome may appear at the plinth — an echo burned into the mechanism from the original attunement. It will offer to provide the confession in the party's place. The Iris will open, but cracked. The anchor is accessible but the release will be unstable. The GM determines the consequence.

## Talara's Absence

[[Talara]] is pulled into the [[Hedge]] by [[The Pharaoh]] at the pyramid's entrance — before she reaches the Calibration Chamber. The party must solve the puzzle without her.

This is deliberate. The answer to the puzzle is built from everything Talara has told the party during her journey — every fae she has found, every court she has explained, every piece of the Chronicle she has shared. Her absence forces the party to become her memory. What they retained from her journey is their answer key.

When Talara and the party reconvene after the Iris is opened, they will have reached the same truths from different directions at the same time. This is the structure of the scene.
