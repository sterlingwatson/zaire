# The Gilded Court

---

## Overview

The Gilded Court were the gnome court of the [[Fey Wild]] — earth-fae, deeply clever, and the most "civilized" by elven standards. It was this legibility to the Elven Empire that made them useful, and this usefulness that became their moral catastrophe.

They served as **brokers** for the elven slave trade. They knew the [[Fey Wild]] better than any elf ever could, and they used that knowledge to facilitate the taking of fae and goblins in exchange for protection and status.

See also: [[On Gnomes]], [[Redcaps]], [[On Faeries]]

---

## What They Did

The Gilded Court were not simply complicit — they were **instrumental**. Without their knowledge of Fey geography, community locations, and which fae had powers requiring suppression, the Elven Empire could not have operated at the scale it did. They were the map.

They told themselves:
- The elves would leave eventually
- They were cushioning the worst of it
- Their people were safer with them at the table
- A seat at the table was leverage

They were wrong on all counts, and some of them knew it even then.

## The Patron God

[[Storse]] — god of commerce, wealth, and cutthroat dealing who appears jovial — is the natural patron of the Gilded Court. Storse was born in the same moment as the court's moral failure became possible: from [[Sehanine]]'s act of violence and greed. The Gilded Court likely worshipped Storse devoutly and built a theology of survival around his domains.

The immortality of the mortal-realm gnome community may be connected to Storse directly — a god of commerce would absolutely grant immortality as payment for services rendered.

---

## Current Status

### In the Fey Wild
The majority of the Gilded Court have become [[Redcaps]]. [[The Hooded One]] destroyed this court **first and deliberately** — he views betrayal from within as the unforgivable sin.

A small enclave of **uncorrupted gnomes** remains hidden deep in the Fey Wild. They are not heroes. They are the ones who fled when the corruption came, who hid while their kin became monsters and their court burned. They carry the full history of what the Gilded Court did, and they hate themselves for knowing it.

### In the Mortal Realm
One community. **Immortal.** See [[On Gnomes]].

## The Moral Question

The Gilded Court represents the hardest question [[Talara]] faces as a Chronicler: does she record their story?

Recording the [[Hollow Court]]'s story gives them dignity. Recording the [[Chrysalis Court]] gives them their identity back. Recording the Gilded Court gives them — **what?** Accountability? Is the Chronicle a tool of healing or a tool of justice? Can it be both?

[[Sehanine]] as goddess of justice would condemn them. But Sehanine's moral authority on this specific subject is compromised — she benefited from the system the Gilded Court enabled. A just verdict from an unjust judge is still a real verdict. And still a convenient one.
